P0089`t[Normal]`tAbstract
P0090`t[Normal]`tEthernaCare is a safety-support mobile application developed to address delayed emergency or death discovery among older adults and other individuals who live alone. This issue is becoming more important as Malaysia moves towards an ageing society and more individuals live separately from their family members. When a person living alone experiences an emergency and is unable to request assistance, the situation may remain unnoticed for a long period. EthernaCare aims to reduce this delay while also helping users prepare and securely communicate their post-death preferences.
P0091`t[Normal]`tThe application uses a gamified virtual companion named Oren to encourage regular interaction. Instead of using a fixed daily check-in, each user can configure a rolling inactivity threshold from one to 168 hours. A successful check-in is recorded when the current threshold window has expired. When a user misses the first window, the application displays a local reminder. The second missed window sends an SMS to the user’s verified phone number, while the third missed window escalates the alert to the selected trusted contact. Oren also includes energy changes, feeding, playing, virtual tokens, owned toys, a shop and weather-based backgrounds. Check-in streaks separately allow users to collect virtual badges and vouchers.
P0092`t[Normal]`tEthernaCare also provides trusted-contact management, emergency location sharing, AI-assisted general guidance and protected Legacy Planning. The Legacy feature allows users to record funeral preferences, Legacy Notes and private documents. After 90 days without a successful check-in, the owner receives a warning and has 24 hours to check in or cancel the process. Otherwise, the verified primary contact may receive protected access for seven days.
P0093`t[Normal]`tThe system was developed using Flutter and Supabase with supporting Edge Functions, scheduled server processing and external SMS, email, weather and AI services.

P0207`t[Heading 2]`tProject Objective(s)
P0208`t[Normal]`tThe first objective of EthernaCare is to provide an engaging safety check-in mechanism for older adults and other individuals who live alone. Users interact with a virtual companion named Oren to record a successful check-in. Instead of requiring a fixed daily interaction, the system allows the user to configure a rolling inactivity threshold between one and 168 hours. This approach provides flexibility for different user routines while still allowing the system to identify prolonged inactivity.
P0209`t[Normal]`tThe second objective is to provide a staged inactivity response process. When the first inactivity window is missed, the application provides a local reminder to the user. When the second window is missed, the system attempts to send an SMS to the user’s verified phone number. If the user remains inactive until the third window, the system escalates the SMS notification to the selected trusted contact. This process is intended to reduce unnecessary alerts while still providing an additional safety layer for users who may require assistance.
P0210`t[Normal]`tThe third objective is to provide secure Legacy Planning functions. Users can record their funeral preferences, write protected Legacy Notes and upload private documents. The system also provides a server-controlled Legacy release process involving owner consent, a unique Legacy UID, a verified primary contact and protected access verification. If the user remains inactive for 90 days, the system warns the owner and provides 24 hours to check in or cancel the process before temporary access may be released to the verified primary contact.
P0211`t[Normal]`tThe fourth objective is to improve regular user engagement through gamification. Oren has an energy level that changes according to inactivity and can be cared for through feeding and playing actions. Users can earn virtual tokens, purchase toys and select owned items for Oren. Check-in streaks separately allow users to unlock virtual badges and vouchers, providing motivation without requiring physical product delivery.
P0212`t[Normal]`tThe fifth objective is to provide accessible general information through AI-assisted guidance. The AI feature helps users find basic information related to elderly care, emergency preparation and funeral arrangements. However, its responses are provided for general informational purposes only and are not medical, legal, emergency or professional advice.
P0213`t[Normal]`tOverall, EthernaCare supports “SDG 3 – Good Health and Well-Being” by providing a practical smartphone-based safety-support application. The system does not diagnose medical conditions, confirm a user’s physical health, guarantee emergency rescue or guarantee successful SMS delivery. Its purpose is to reduce the possibility that prolonged inactivity remains unnoticed and to support communication between users and their trusted contacts.

P0273`t[Heading 2]`tAging Population in Malaysia
P0274`t[Heading 3]`tDemographic Shift Toward an Ageing Society
P0275`t[Normal]`tMalaysia is moving toward an ageing nation as the percentage of citizens aged 60 and above continues to increase. The Department of Statistics Malaysia (Current Population Estimates, Malaysia, 2023, 2023) projects that older adults will make up around 15% of the population by 2030. This change is mainly influenced by longer life expectancy and lower birth rates. The transition creates new challenges in healthcare planning, elderly care services, and community support systems.
P0276`t[Heading 3]`tIncrease in Elderly Living Alone
P0277`t[Normal]`tThe increase in elderly individuals living alone is closely linked to broader demographic and social transitions. According to the World Report on Ageing and Health by the World Health Organization (2015), although more generations within a family may now be alive at the same time due to increased life expectancy, these generations are increasingly living separately. In many countries, the proportion of older people living alone is rising dramatically. For example, in several European countries, more than 40% of women aged 65 and above live alone. Even in societies with strong traditions of multigenerational living, such as Japan and India, extended family households are becoming less common. Declining fertility rates mean there are fewer younger family members available to provide care, while changing gender roles have reduced the traditional caregiving capacity within families. Smaller household sizes may limit reciprocal care arrangements and increase the risk of poverty. Furthermore, older adults living alone may face higher risks of social isolation and suicide. Although many older people prefer to remain in their own homes and communities for as long as possible, these structural changes suggest that traditional family-based care models are becoming less sustainable in the modern era (World Health Organization, 2015).
P0278`t[Heading 2]`tTechnology concepts
P0279`t[Heading 3]`tSmartphone-Based Passive Monitoring
P0280`t[Normal]`tSmartphone-based monitoring systems use built-in sensors and activity logs to observe user behavior without requiring constant manual input (Wang, 2014). This passive approach allows the system to detect unusual patterns such as prolonged inactivity, which may indicate potential emergencies. Because the monitoring process runs automatically in the background, it reduces the need for continuous interaction and can operate without disturbing users.
P0281`t[Heading 3]`tDaily Check-In Monitoring Systems
P0282`t[Normal]`tDaily check-in monitoring systems are used in safety and well-being applications to verify that users are active through simple interactions with a mobile device. Instead of continuously collecting sensor data, gamified check-in systems utilize virtual pets or interactive elements to maintain user engagement. By requiring a tactile interaction like 'petting' an on-screen character, the system ensures the user is physically capable and responsive, serving as a more interactive form of passive monitoring (Rashidi & Mihailidis, 2013).
P0283`t[Heading 3]`tWearable or Sensor Monitoring
P0284`t[Heading 3]`tWearable and sensor-based monitoring systems are commonly used in elderly care technologies to continuously track health conditions and daily activities. These systems typically involve wearable devices such as smartwatches or sensor bands that collect physiological and movement data, including heart rate, walking patterns, and sleep activity. The collected data can be analyzed to identify abnormal patterns such as falls, prolonged inactivity, or sudden changes in physical behaviour (Patel et al., 2012) .
P0285`t[Heading 3]`tDuolingo and Behavioral Gamification
P0286`t[Normal]`tModern gamification success stories, most notably Duolingo, demonstrate that habit formation is driven by emotional engagement and loss aversion. According to research on Duolingo’s growth strategy, the use of a persistent mascot (Duo the Owl) and a daily "streak" mechanic transforms a high-effort task into a "sticky" daily routine (Hasan, 2026). By quantifying consistency, users feel a sense of ownership over their progress—a psychological phenomenon known as the Endowment Effect.
P0287`t[Normal]`tEthernaCare adapts these proven tactics by replacing standard check-in prompts with a virtual cat companion. By leveraging the emotional bond between the user and the digital pet, the system ensures high retention rates, as users are motivated to "check-in" to maintain their pet's well-being and their own reward progress.
P0288`t[Heading 2]`tTechnology Acceptance Among the Elderly
P0289`t[Heading 3]`tSmartphone Accessibility Among Elderly Users
P0290`t[Normal]`tIn recent years, smartphone ownership among older adults has increased significantly. According to the Pew Research Center (2021), smartphone adoption among individuals aged 65 and above has grown substantially compared to previous decades. The increased accessibility of smartphones provides opportunities to develop mobile-based solutions for elderly safety and monitoring. Since many elderly individuals already own smartphones, mobile applications can serve as cost-effective tools without requiring additional hardware (Pew Research Center, 2025).
P0291`t[Heading 3]`tPerceived Usefulness and Ease of Use
P0292`t[Normal]`tRecent studies show that perceived usefulness and ease of use remain strong predictors of technology adoption among older adults. Research on digital health and mobile monitoring systems indicates that elderly users are more likely to adopt applications when they believe the system improves their safety and supports independent living (Anderson et al., 2020). Confidence in smartphone usage and prior experience with digital tools also influence acceptance. When applications are perceived as complicated or unnecessary, adoption rates decrease.
P0293`t[Heading 3]`tImportance of User-Friendly Design
P0294`t[Normal]`tUsability plays a critical role in elderly technology adoption. Studies on mobile health applications emphasize that simple navigation, readable fonts, clear instructions, and minimal interaction steps significantly improve user satisfaction and long-term engagement (Dusseljee-Peute et al., 2018). Complex menus and small interface elements may discourage elderly users. Therefore, systems designed for older adults should prioritize clarity, accessibility, and reduced cognitive effort.

P0300`t[Heading 2]`tSimilar Existing Systems in the market
P0301`t[Heading 3]`tApple Emergency SOS
P0302`t[Normal]`tApple Emergency SOS is a built-in smartphone feature that allows users to quickly contact emergency services and notify selected emergency contacts. The system can be activated using hardware button combinations, and it automatically shares the user’s location with emergency responders and trusted contacts (Apple Inc., n.d.). The main feature is immediate emergency calling with real-time location transmission. It is integrated into iOS devices and depends on manual activation by the user.
P0303`t[Normal]`tThis system is related to EthernaCare because both involve emergency alerts and contact notification. The difference is that EthernaCare uses a daily greeting check-in mechanism to confirm the user's status instead of requiring manual triggering. In addition, EthernaCare integrates post-death planning and digital document storage, which are not included in Apple Emergency SOS.
P0305`t[Heading 3]`tCarePredict
P0306`t[Normal]`tCarePredict is a wearable-based monitoring system designed for elderly individuals, particularly those in assisted living facilities. The system uses a wearable device equipped with sensors to monitor daily activities such as eating, sleeping, walking, and bathroom visits. It applies artificial intelligence and behavior pattern analysis to detect anomalies that may indicate health or safety risks (CarePredict, n.d.).
P0307`t[Normal]`tThe main feature is continuous activity tracking through wearable sensors combined with AI-based behavioral analytics. The strength of the system lies in its detailed monitoring capabilities. However, it requires users to wear a dedicated device and is often implemented in institutional care settings.
P0308`t[Normal]`tEthernaCare shares the concept of behavior monitoring but differs by using smartphone-based passive detection rather than a wearable device. It is also designed for elderly individuals living independently at home rather than those in assisted living environments.
P0310`t[Heading 2]`tLimitation of the project

P0315`t[Heading 3]`tExternal Provider and Internet Dependency
P0316`t[Normal]`tSeveral functions depend on internet connectivity and third-party providers. P0364`t[Normal]`tOne major problem faced by elderly individuals living alone is the risk of not being noticed when something happens to them, such as illness, falls, or sudden health emergencies. P0472`t[Heading 3]`tArchitecture Justification
P0473`t[Normal]`tThe three-tier architecture is selected for the EthernaCare system to ensure a clear separation between user interface, system logic, and data management. This design improves system maintainability, scalability, and flexibility while supporting integration with external services such as location tracking and AI assistance. P0474`t[Normal]`tPresentation Layer
P0475`t[Normal]`tThe Presentation layer is responsible for handling all user interactions through interfaces such as the Home Page UI, Check-In History UI, Contacts UI, Rewards UI, Profile UI, and AI Assistance UI. P0476`t[Normal]`tBusiness Logic Layer
P0477`t[Normal]`tThe Business Logic layer serves as the core processing unit of the system, where all major functionalities such as Virtual Petting Check-In, Emergency Response, Contact and Location Management, Rewarding System, and AI Assistance are implemented. By centralizing all logic in this layer, the system ensures better organization, easier debugging, and improved scalability. It also allows developers to modify or extend system behavior without affecting the user interface or database structure.
P0478`t[Normal]`tData Access Layer
P0479`t[Normal]`tThe Data Access layer is responsible for managing all interactions with the database and external storage systems, including repositories, authentication, real-time tracking, and file storage. It acts as an intermediary between the Business Logic layer and the Supabase database, ensuring that data is stored, retrieved, and managed securely and efficiently. This separation prevents direct access to the database from higher layers, improving data integrity and security. Additionally, it provides flexibility to change or upgrade the database or storage system in the future without impacting other parts of the application.

P0484`t[Normal]`tMaintainability: Changes in one layer do not necessarily impact other layers, thus simplifying the maintenance. (Geeksforgeeks, 2025)
